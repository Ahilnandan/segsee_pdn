Supply-noise co-design: compiled results

Compiled 2026-10-07. All experiments in pdn_work/. Droop numbers are p99.9 per-cycle die-voltage drop unless marked “worst”.

Bottom line

Three pieces combine. A parallel supply, where an off-chip buck carries DC and a slow-switching FIVR handles transients. A small on-die active bank. One droop predictor in the issue stage that both holds the heaviest instructions and places background work. Together they cut p99.9 droop by 55–62% against a conventional 140 MHz FIVR. The FIVR runs 4× slower, system efficiency rises 2–4 points, and performance cost is 0.04–0.5% on single-thread BOOM.

Reordering instructions on its own does not reduce droop; it has a hard ceiling of a few percent. The lever is when work issues, decided by a forward prediction from the measured supply.

Single-thread BOOM, full traces, scheduler co-simulated cycle by cycle inside the Pulsim switching circuit. Each bar adds one piece to the one above it. Apparatus

Core and traces

  • BOOM PaperMatchBoomV3Config RTL (Verilator): 8-wide dispatch, 96-entry IQ, 384 ROB. BACT_CAND trace logs every ready candidate per cycle.
  • Replay with real dependencies, the real port map and age priority. Oldest-first reproduces hardware timing exactly (dhrystone 87,312 = 87,312 cycles).
  • Power tokens in Chisel (8-bit ROM per uop: 4-bit amplitude, 4-bit duration), verified bit-exact against curmodel.py.
  • SMT-4/8 model of the Fu et al. ICPP'08 machine (8-wide, unified 96-entry IQ, ICOUNT) built from BOOM trace slices.

Power delivery

  • Pulsim (C++ MNA) switching simulation at 50 ps steps, cross-validated against the closed-form model to 1–2%.
  • Off-chip 4-phase buck: 500 kHz, per-core share (K = 250) of a 100 A VR, average-current-mode control.
  • FIVR: 16 phases, natural-sampled double-edge PWM, type-3 loop at 78 MHz / 43° with 0.6 ns delay (Burton APEC'14 class).
  • Package 507 pH / 79.6 mΩ, die 5 nF. Scheduler runs inside the circuit and sees sensed state every cycle.

1 · Supply topology

Direct path only, FIVR only, and both in parallel feeding the same die node. In the parallel case the off-chip loop nulls the FIVR's average current, so the FIVR carries only 1–7% of DC current.

Conduction losses are simulated from source currents. FIVR switching loss is calibrated at 2.72 pJ per phase-cycle so that FIVR-only at 140 MHz is 90% efficient. The off-chip VR has 5% switching loss and the 1.8 V rail is made at 90%. At 35 MHz the inductor must scale up, or ripple conduction loss cancels the switching savings.

3 · Does reordering help?

Tested on single-thread BOOM, on an SMT-4/8 model with Fu-et-al.-level choice, and with a perfect-model predictive selector. Every policy issues the same number of ops as oldest-first.

TestResult
Single-thread, minimise-bank-need reorder (T)No droop change (150.8 → 150.5 mV)
Supply-following / spread-spectrum reorderMixed; resonance-band energy unchanged (mm 17.8% → 17.9%)
SMT-8, minimise-bank-need reorderBank charge −14 to −22% at ≤0.1% runtime change; droop ±3%
SMT-8, state-of-charge-aware selection, half-size bankBank shortfall −28 to −76%
SMT, predictive selection with exact PDN modelp99.9 within ±5% (best −5.6%), worst case mixed
Under tuned predictive holds (GA)Tie with oldest-first (0.1% fitness)

Why there is a ceiling. The top 1% of current steps are mostly issue-count rises (+2.2 to +2.4 ops/cycle at ~80 pC each), which a work-conserving selector cannot change. What it can move must still issue within about one resonance period (~10 cycles), so charge per period is nearly conserved. Reordering takes pressure off the bank, but does not reduce droop.

4 · Predictive holds

Each cycle, predict die voltage 13 cycles ahead from the supply's measured impulse response, the current history, the committed tails of in-flight ops, and the candidate picks. If the predicted dip exceeds the target, hold the heaviest picks, one at a time, until it does not. Target = 0.68 × oldest-first p99.9 (GA-tuned). A starvation guard forces an op through after 19 holds.

Redundant execution (DMR) as scheduled work

Every integer op is re-executed within a deadline. Overdue checks reserve ports, which models commit blocking. Coverage is 100% in all rows; the checks add 20–34% charge. Percentages are vs the same core without DMR.

Full integer DMR, which normally costs about 40% more droop, ends up with less droop than the unprotected core. Opportunistic overshoot-only re-execution was neutral (±3%, 1–5% coverage). Filler current lands in the next trough of the supply's 7–10-cycle ring.

7 · Earlier groundwork

  • The HTIF program-load phase was 72–87% of every raw trace and had to be stripped. Real issue contention is 9–47% of active cycles (mean choice set 2–3.5).
  • Closed-loop sensed tokens are required; open-loop token models were about 2× optimistic on slowdown.
  • Real switched-cap charge pump decomposed per execution unit: CSR needs the most decap despite the lowest mean current.
  • Meng & Saleh JSSC 2009 active decap: all five gain-equation examples re-derived exactly.
  • Dummy fill (THF 200) alone: −8 to −16% droop for +3–4% energy.

9 · Caveats

  • The per-uop current model is approximate (±30–40% absolute). It was rebuilt after the original library was lost. Relative comparisons are the reliable part.
  • The parallel supply's result relies on 16-phase double-edge PWM passing control content above fsw. With a stricter modulator it may weaken.
  • Switches are ideal. Switching loss is calibrated, not simulated, and PWM chatter at 4× L (~1.6× transitions) is not charged.
  • SMT results come from a replay model built on single-thread BOOM traces, not SMT RTL. Single-thread results use real BOOM ports and dependencies.
  • The predictor is a 256-tap convolution plus a 13×10 effect matrix. It needs a low-order IIR and a per-token droop-signature ROM to fit the issue-select path; hardware cost is not yet measured.
  • Supply is a per-core share on one lumped die node: no multi-core rail and no spatial grid. The 1.8 V rail in the parallel case is ideal.
  • Workloads are qsort, dhrystone and mm. There are no SPEC runs and no power-virus stressmarks yet.
